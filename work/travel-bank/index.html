Building Trust to Reduce Onboarding Drop-Offs

In 2019 Texas Capital Bank (TCB), a regional bank with over $40B in managed assets, had just launched Bask Bank, a new “digital attacker” bank that offered a travel-oriented savings account that accrued American Airlines miles instead of interest.

While the launch was driving prospects to the website, they were dropping off at high rates while onboarding flow. And for customers that complete onboarding? Their initial deposits were below target.I heard about these issues during a sprint review and volunteered to help, joining the Product Manager and Technical Lead.

I led an effort to research drop-off drivers and redesign onboarding to reduce drop-offs and improve initial deposits. We had 2 key goals:

  • How might we reduce onboarding drop-offs?
  • How might we improve average initial deposits?

In X months, we won approval of the redesign from leadership and implemented the first wave of enhancements. Leadership reported a reduction in drop-off rates and a bonus reduction in customer service calls. I also contributed to create a data-driven design culture by creating an onboarding drop-offs dashboard for step-by-step visibility and serve as a source-of-truth for my team and leadership.

Uncovering Drop-Off Drivers

I couldn't talk to customers directly so we worked with proxies. I interviewed a Customer Support Lead, reviewed call logs, audited the current-state, and analyzed other leading “digital attacker” competitors. We found:

  1. Bask had Little Brand Credibility

    Customers were interested enough to click to through the site and into onboarding, but Bask didn't have the same leash as other digital-first banks. Customers carried doubt in the back of their minds. To make up for this, Bask needed to look and feel like it was on the same tier as its competitor digital-native bank such as Chime and Marcus to appear more credible.

    "Is this a real bank? I’ve never heard of them before”

  2. Jarring Transition Between Website and Onboarding

    The website was modern and polished, but onboarding had a completely different, outdated experience. It felt like being taken to a dingy backroom to finish account creation after seeing a nice showroom.

  3. Asking for Deposits Too Soon

    The current-state tried to move too fast. It asked users to commit to an initial deposit too early in the flow, before login credentials and before a funding bank was set. The thought process was that Bask needed to capture the deposit as soon as possible, but this spooked customers who needed more time to get comfortable.

Building User Trust to Reduce Drop-Offs

I designed a rework of onboarding to help customers feel more comfortable and reduce drop-offs. And for deposits, the more trust built, the more users would feel depositing at the start. To facilitate conversations with engineering team, I redesigned the onboarding process end-to-end to create a north star:

Ease Users In Rather Than Ask for Deposits Upfront

Onboarding should build rapport and ease users in by making small asks first to get users comfortable. Asking for a big commitment, a deposit, was scaring some users away. I moved the initial deposit later in the flow, right after they connected their funding bank. In addition, I added a step to collect the user's frequent flier # so that we could remind them of the value they could get and ensure they would receive their rewards.

We hypothesized that since users are already wary, asking for a big commitment could scare them away. I moved the initial deposit later in the process after they connected their funding bank so they would know that a transfer was possible before deciding on a deposit. In addition, I added an optional step to gather the user's frequent flier # to lean on partner credibility and reduce the likelihood they forget to connect their account later and be unable to receive their miles.

On Brand Onboarding

The home page and onboarding felt like two completely different companies created them. We needed to make the Bask experience feel as one single entity. I created components to match the visual language of the website and used those to redesign Onboarding to keep consistent between the two.

Transparency

Users needed to fill in a larger number of fields to complete onboarding, including a set of Know Your Customer questions. To fight field fatigue and data privacy concerns, I added a short explainer on each step to explain why and how the information would be used and a progress tracker to break down the process into smaller milestones.

Increasing Initial Deposits

New customers could earn a hefty bonus for maintaining over certain balances. While the rewards tiers were mentioned on the website, users had to remember the reward tiers in the previous experience.

I saw an opportunity for a win-win. I designed a new initial deposit screen that re-surfaced deposit and reward tiers to nudge users toward higher initial deposits, and have one less reason to go back to the website (and potentially drop-off!).

Growing Data-Driven Design

I partnered with engineering to implement the first wave of features to build towards the north star I designed. In the first release, we the visual update and reordered onboarding steps. Leadership saw drop-offs fall and encouraged us to continue building a more data-driven approach to drive product decisions.

Building Quantitative and Qualitative Capabilities

Bask could see how many entered onboarding and how many accounts were created, but didn't have visibility into drop-offs at each step in onboarding. I created a funnel chart to track progress within onboarding so my team and leadership could also track drop-offs at a step-by-step level to understand where users were dropping off the most - where we might make the biggest impact. I shared this with Bask leadership as well so they would have the same source-of-truth as my team would.

To grow their qualitative capabilities, I researched remote testing platforms and recommended UserTesting.com, which the client licensed. I identified key questions and designed tests to validate new Bask features before launch.
