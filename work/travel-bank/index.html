Building Trust to Reduce Onboarding Drop-Offs

In 2019 Texas Capital Bank (TCB), a regional bank with over $40B in managed assets, had just launched Bask Bank, a new “digital attacker” bank that offered a travel-oriented savings account that accrued American Airlines miles instead of interest. While the launch was successful driving prospects to the website, they were dropping off at high rates in the onboarding flow and never complete creating their account. And for customers that complete onboarding? Their initial deposits were below target.

I heard about these issues during a sprint review and volunteered to help, joining the Product Manager and Technical Lead. I led an effort to research drop-off drivers and redesign onboarding to reduce drop-offs and improve initial deposits.

In X months, we won approval of the redesign from leadership and implemented the first wave of enhancements Leadership reported a reduction in drop-off rates and a bonus reduction in customer service calls. I created a metrics dashboard to pinpoint drop-offs step-by-step in onboarding.

Uncovering Drop-Off Drivers

I couldn't talk to customers directly so I relied on proxies. I interviewed a Customer Support Lead, reviewed call logs, audited the current-state, and analyzed competitors. These were the issues:

  1. Bask had Little Brand Credibility

    Customers were interested enough to click to through the site and into onboarding, but Bask didn't have the same leash as other digital-first banks. Customers carried doubt in the back of their minds. To make up for this, Bask needed to look and feel like it was on the same tier as its competitor digital-native bank such as Chime and Marcus to appear more credible.

    "Is this a real bank? I’ve never heard of them before”

  2. Jarring Transition Between Website and Onboarding

    The website was modern and polished, but onboarding had a completely different, outdated experience. It felt like being taken to a dingy backroom to finish the deal after being taken through a nice showroom.

  3. Asking for Deposits Too Soon

    Onboarding needs to be efficient, but the current-state tried to move too quickly. The existing flow asked prospects to commit to an initial deposit early in the flow, before login credentials and before a funding bank was set. The thinking was that Bask needed to capture the deposit as soon as possible and move through the process as fast as possible, but even asking for this number didn't mean that the transfer could even happen right then. More steps needed to happen.

Building User Trust to Reduce Drop-Offs

I hypothesized that the following changes would help prospects overcome their skepticism and complete account creation. For deposits, the more trust built, the more users would feel depositing at the start.

Demonstrate Value before Asking for Commitment

Since users would be interacting with Bask for the first time I reframed onboarding as an opportunity to build rapport and ease users in by making small asks first to get them get comfortable and see that onboarding was similar to other banks they might be familiar with, and that we would guide them through key steps needed for them to earn their miles, the reason they're here.

We hypothesized that since users are already wary, asking for a big commitment could scare them away. I moved the initial deposit later in the process after they connected their funding bank so they would know that a transfer was possible before deciding on a deposit. In addition, I added an optional step to gather the user's frequent flier # to lean on partner credibility and reduce the likelihood they forget to connect their account later and be unable to receive their miles.

On Brand Onboarding

The home page and onboarding felt like two completely different companies made them. Which was true, two vendors were responsible. However, customers needed to see both as one unified experience. Onboarding needed to share the same visual language as the website to avoid spooking customers. Some of its competitors could get by with a brand name bank's backing, but Bask only had its product offering and design.

Increasing Initial Deposits

New customers could earn a hefty bonus for maintaining over certain balances. While the rewards tiers were mentioned on the website, users had to remember the reward tiers in the previous experience.

I saw an opportunity for a win-win. I designed a new initial deposit screen that re-surfaced deposit and reward tiers to nudge users toward higher initial deposits, and have one less reason to go back to the website (and potentially drop-off!).

Outcomes

The engineering team implemented the enhancements in waves. After the first set hit prod, leadership reported a reduction in drop-off rates and a bonus reduction in customer service calls.

We continued to add enhancements until the vendor platform could no longer be customized. The client used the designs to inform a RFP to find a vendor that could implement the proposed redesign.

Growing Data-Driven Design

After seeing drop-offs fall, the client wanted to continue tying feature development to business metrics.

Quantitiative

Bask could track overall drop-offs but didn't have visibility into drop-offs at each step so I created a funnel chart in Amplitude, a product analytics platform, to track progress within onboarding and provide a senior leadership and the onboarding team with a source-of-truth.

Our biggest opportunity was right at the start. After users saw the first onboarding screen, over half would leave.

Qualitative - User Testing

To gather more qualititative insights we brought on the UserTesting.com platform and ran tests on future features.

COVID-19 Reduces Travel Demand

Once the COVID-19 pandemic began, demand for travel dipped and interest in the product dipped. Unfortunately the full onboarding vision was never fully built out.
