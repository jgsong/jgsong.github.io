Ghost Busting Fake College Applications

In 2025 I led a redesign of a student application fraud detection tool for a major college system that serves over 2 million students. Fraudsters would submit fake student apps and to scam win financial aid. These those ghost students would Professors and real students would sometimes show up to near-empty classrooms because of these ghost students.

Analysts would use the application fraud detection tool to review student enrollment application metadata and flag fraudulent applications. Our goal was to improve the speed and accuracy of analysts to flag fraudulent applications so that ML algorithm would have better data to train off of and automatically detect fraud with better accuracy, a virtuous cycle.

Colleges were committed to fighting fraud but were struggling to keep up. Applications had increased by over 2 million from the year before and many didn't have dedicated staff to review applications. Employees were doing the best to stem the tide on top of their day-to-day responsibilites.

In X months, I led a team with a product designer and product architect to update the tool. We worked with a 18 person working group committee of power users and the engineering team to create a solution that would improve user workflows and play well with existing algorithmic fraud detection efforts and technical constraints.

Location is Everything

There were more than a hundred data fields shown to analysts for each profile, but they relied on just a handful of key fields before making the decision whether to flag an application as fraudulent or not. They weren't easy to scan and were scattered across the page. We could make the most impact by surfacing those to the top and put commonly compared fields next to each other.

Explaining the Fraud Score

Each application had a fraud score assigned by the system that analysts considered useless. Some scores were above 100% and scores often didn't line up with their assessments and they had no idea how it was put together.

We needed to build trust in it and show how it's put together so analysts can "compare notes" with the score.

I led a working session with the engineer who created the algorithm. I broke down all the different factors that went into the score. One challenge was that we didn't have visiblity into the ML portion of the algorithm. Also, couldn't show analysts exactly how the score was put together to prevent bad actors on the inside from reverse engineering the score and using it to bypass the system
